# Moth instrument and dance concept pack

The Moth plays a wind instrument built into her left gauntlet, then drops her
hands straight into a dance move. Use these sheets to model the instrument,
pose the fingers and block out the dance on four beats. The
[instrument and dance plan](../../../plans/instrument-play.md#the-concept-art-brief)
owns the play requirements. The [Moth concept pack](../moth-concept-pack/README.md)
owns her proportions and construction. The stage uses the warm stone, cream
modules and deep teal supports of the [Canyon Workshop direction](../valley-and-gallery.md#selected-art-treatment).

## Model the gauntlet and hands

Reuse the squared left gauntlet and its two outer vent recesses. An ivory rib
divides each recess into two fingertip seats. A small bore in each seat takes
one finger pad to seal. The raised cuffs leave the wrists clear. A blower in
the elbowward end takes air through the louvred intake and feeds the vents
and the acoustic outlet below the finger deck. The blower and duct fit in
the outer wall and leave a clear tunnel for the forearm through the shell.
Keep the original right gauntlet.

The cyan rim at each seat lights when the blower is active and its bore is
open. Covering that bore darkens its rim while the other seats retain their
own states. From elbow to wrist the shell carries the louvred intake, the
three-segment amber breath bar, the two-by-two vent deck and the cyan wrist
ring. The ring shows module power and stays lit when the blower is off.

Aim the right finger pads at the bores in the left forearm's local frame.
The right palm floats above the deck. The left hand can open and close
freely beyond the cuff. Use charcoal segments and visible pivots, keeping
the joints clear of the cuffs at the extremes. The angle marks give a
starting range for a rig study. The close contact and motion checks in the
plan establish the usable range on the built hand. A sleeve liner supports
the smaller shell on the pilot's left forearm and leaves the same approach
available to her bare right hand.

The torso and left upper arm hide the vent lights in the four-metre chase
study. At that distance the pose reads before the fingering does. Check
vent visibility with the built rig and chase camera.

## Block out the beats

The travelling step lifts a knee, plants wide, gathers with a shoulder
accent, and finishes in a broad, open shape. Let the weight arrive over each
boot before the next one moves. The spin winds up, pivots, sweeps low and
settles. Its boot and the opposite hand open a clear line across the body
on the strike beat. The leap compresses, travels in the air, lands on both
boots and ends upright with open palms. The comic accent comes from the
size of the movements and a clean stop on the beat.

Keep the thruster pods closed and the boot lights steady. Read the step and
spin from the chase camera, then use the side and rear cameras to check
support and joint clearance. For the leap, use the foot shadows to separate
take-off, flight and the landing strike. Use the crowd view to judge the
leading boot, open arm and hood at distance. The ground diagrams give
order and direction for blocking. The authored move supplies the distances
and timings.
