# Barn swallow reference

The visual standard for the debug world's barn swallow, the perched male
*Hirundo rustica* on the reservoir pavilion's west rail, built as the native
SDF creation in [birds.puck](../../../worlds/debug/birds.puck) and read in the
selected [valley-and-gallery](valley-and-gallery.md) treatment. It exists
because six rebuilds were each judged against a different idea of a swallow:
this page fixes the proportions in centimetres, the silhouette each preview view
must show, and the palette, so a builder and a critic measure the same target.
It holds no images of its own; the references it cites are the images.

What the sources agree on: a bird about 18 cm long of which the tail is more
than half (tail 9–11 cm in a male, the streamers 2–7 cm beyond the inner
feathers), on a 12–13 cm wing, weighing about 20 g, with an 11 mm tarsus and
a skull 3 cm long. It is a flying machine: long wing, long tail, small head
and bill, tiny legs, and a body no deeper than a third of its own length.

## The stylized target

The debug world's swallow is a male in the nominate colours, drawn in the
treatment's simplified forms: one shaped body, held values, crisp painted
markings. Stylization sharpens what says "swallow" (the taper, the points, the
fork, the small flat head) and never adds bulk (a round chest, a domed head, a
broad wing). The numbers are in the bird's own units, centimetres, as
`birds.puck` authors them in metres; the placement draws it at 1.5 times that.
Ratios are against **L**, the length from the bill's tip to the tail's root
(the body length), which is 7.6 cm.

The real bird these sit inside: L about 7.2–7.5 cm (an 18 cm bird less a
10.8 cm male tail), chest depth about 2.6 cm, wing chord 12.5 cm, tail
10.8 cm, tarsus 1.1 cm, skull 3 cm. The target lengthens nothing and shortens
only the folded wing's reach, so the tips stop at the fork as the photographs
show rather than at the flattened chord's end.

## Silhouette targets, view by view

Each is checkable on `puck mesh preview`'s views (author frame, azimuth 0 the
front, +90 the bird's own left, elevation 90 from above), where every triangle
shows its palette albedo and paints are omitted, so these are statements about
form alone. The critic's hard fails (a seam, a tear, an open edge drawn red, a
back face drawn magenta, a facet, a fragment) apply to every view before any
of these is read.

| View | The silhouette must show |
|---|---|
| Side (`left`, `right`) | One convex back line from the crown over the nape to the rump, the crown no more than 0.3 cm proud of it. A deeper convex line from the bill's base down the throat and breast to the belly and up to the tail root. Chest depth about one third of L. The folded wing's inner half lies flat on the flank and is paint, so the preview's form shows a plain flank there; the primaries a long dark blade emerging beside the leg and running to a point past the rump: its lower edge straight, no step or notch along it, its tip an included angle of at most 15° about 0.7 L behind the tail root. The streamers continuing at least 0.4 L beyond the wingtips, each one tapering blade. The tail's axis within 15° of the body's. A visible shank of at most 0.6 cm; toes over the edge. |
| Front (`front`) | The head narrower than the chest (two thirds), sitting on it with no neck step. The chest round, as wide as it is deep. Nothing wider than the chest: no shoulder or wrist knob outside the body's outline. The streamers two thin lines hanging below. The wingtips hidden behind the body or barely showing at its sides. |
| Top (`top`) | A torpedo: widest a third of the way back, tapering to the tail root. The two wing blades leaving the flanks and crossing in an X behind the rump, each tip a separate point. The fork's V and both streamers visible past the crossing. |
| Back (`back`) | The back an upright oval; the two blades' upper edges converging to the crossing; two distinct pointed tips, one over the other. The tail fan below with its V notch; the streamers two separate lines. |
| Below and behind (`below-behind`, azimuth 170, elevation −25) | The belly's underside one smooth surface. The tail fan's underside a flat plate with the V notch between the halves, no ridge where the halves meet at the root. The crossed tips above it, each pointed. The feet gripping the rail's edge, the hind toe behind it. |
| Three-quarter front (`front-left`, `front-right`) | The forehead sloping into the bill with no corner at the gape; the breast's curve into the belly unbroken by the wing's root. |
| Three-quarter back (`back-left`, `back-right`) | The wing blade's upper edge lying along the back's side with no plate standing proud; the tips crossing; the far streamer visible beside the near one. |

## What the engine can hold

A static placement at 1.5 times bird size bakes at a finest cell of 2 mm in
world metres (`WorldPlacementStamper.FinestDetail`); `puck mesh audit --cpu`
reports the swallow's one group at a 1.4 mm cell, which is under 1 mm in the
bird's units. So in the bird's centimetres: a blade is at least 0.26 cm
thick, a fillet or crease wider than 0.2 cm or else left as a hard union (an
exact intersection line bakes clean; a fillet narrower than the cell bakes
as stairs), and a pointed tip rounds to the blade's dilation radius, about
0.13 cm, which is the engine's limit rather than the sculpt's. A warped shape
(`flare`, `shear`, `bumps`) bakes alone on a static placement
(`WorldPlacementStamper.BakesAlone`), so the body's taper comes from a
primitive that tapers on its own, never from a warp. The construction that
meets this page is recorded in `birds.puck`'s comments.
