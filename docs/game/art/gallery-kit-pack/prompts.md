## Display plinth

```text
Paint a new landscape concept painting for the modeller of Puck's Canyon workshop museum. Use all five supplied paintings. First is the owner's baseline B, authoritative for silhouette, colour and painted light. Second establishes the entrance atrium and floor edges. Third establishes the window fan and branched support construction. Fourth establishes the shape commons, floor pads and clear aisles. Fifth establishes the plinth and its controls. Use the particular details that belong to this subject. Keep B's chunky, slightly futuristic, rounded cream and ochre modules, recessed deep teal, inset turquoise glass, orange fan plates and terracotta. Preserve the hand-painted animation-background treatment: broad gouache planes, decisive cel shadows, restrained broken-colour brushwork and warm bounced canyon light. Aim for forms a modeller can carve from simple solids, including the thickness at junctions. Maintain continuous supports, clear routes and plausible proportions. Landscape format. No words, numbers, annotations, arrows, measurement marks, lettering, logos or watermarks anywhere. No photographic materials, CAD outlines, dense greebles or pasted source panels.

Paint a landscape character-and-prop scale sheet, on the same warm ivory field throughout. Deliberately asymmetrical two-column layout. LEFT HALF: a large standing adult museum visitor seen from the side next to the front elevation of a LOW exhibit plinth. This human-plus-box silhouette is the centrepiece. Draw the figure almost the full height of this panel, allowing generous margins. The visitor is 1.75 m tall. From where their shoes touch the floor, the top of the exhibit should reach ONLY TO MID-THIGH, well below their belt. The front of the plinth measures about one standing person tall laid sideways (1.8 m) and ONLY 0.75 m high. Keep the adult and box in exactly the same elevation and on the same baseline. Allow this column more than half the sheet if it helps their proportions. RIGHT HALF: two SEPARATE smaller studies of this exact empty plinth, an assembled three-quarter view above and a matching side elevation below, clearly spaced. The three-quarter view reveals a luminous thin circular turntable ring inset within a broad cream bevelled cap, visibly circular in plan. Make all views agree. Shape-workbench-v2 is the object: rounded-rectangle ochre body panels with vertical joints over a recessed deep-teal foot, chamfered cream cap above. A solid cream wedge lectern tilts back from the FRONT and attaches to the front skirt, with a dark flat screen, two brass knobs, and a separate round dial. Screen shows simple solid shape icons only. Clear profiles, controls distinct on the face, joints and footing visible, studio light. No object on the turntable. No written marks, no height line, no labels.
```

Open they make one broad fan, gathering the visible orange surface of the source into a handful of broad colour planes. Closed the SAME slats nest behind the foremost slat like an ordinary folding hand fan. Tips all stop on the same circular arc about a single stationary dark hub. Keep the bay rectangular and flat to the picture plane, avoid an oriel or protruding upper-storey balcony. Put the scale person next to the window on the same floor. Glazing section should show the hub bracket fixed to the central frame rather than hanging in unsupported glass.

Small ordinary visitors at 1.75 m scale looking or operating a station, no hero. Warm bounce from polished terra tiles, distant cool canyon shadows, broad graphic pools of daylight and clear playable foreground, the inviting quiet of a workshop you can explore.
