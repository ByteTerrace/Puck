# Gallery kit visual review

Codex painted and judged these images under the artist-as-judge brief. I checked each against the five inputs for attachment, motion, weight, agreement between views, perspective and compositing. The people are ordinary visitors. Their fixed reference is a standing height of 1.75 m. The numbers in the [modeller's guide](README.md) set the blockout dimensions. [Concept art review](../README.md#concept-art-review)

## Display plinth

**Accepted the third painting.** In the first front view, the body measured about one and a half visitor heights across. The second still put the cap near the hip. Both would have encouraged the modeller to oversize the station. Giving the visitor and front elevation most of the sheet brought the cap down to the thigh.

The visitor and foot share a baseline. The ochre panel joints stop at the cream bevel above and the recessed toe below. The side elevation shows the solid wedge supporting the controls. In the three-quarter view, the two brass knobs and separate dial sit on the inclined face, outside the ring's sweep. The screen icons retain their order between views. The right-hand studies have space around their silhouettes.

## Branched pier and arcade

**Accepted the first painting.** Both openings spring from the flat-faced shafts and resolve into a continuous bearing under the slab. The person stands inside the left opening with clear headroom. The side section shows how far the floor projects beyond the upright. In the enlarged oblique view the chamfer catches light on its own plane, and three short brass strips sit beside the vertical joint. Both views preserve the dark reveal under the cream edge. The section cuts are clear and the structure is fixed throughout.

## Window bay

**Accepted the second painting.** The first showed two broad orange plates closing into a much narrower shape. It gave the modeller no joint or overlap to account for the change in width. The revision shows the radial edges when spread and the exposed edges of the stack when gathered.

The hub stays at the same mullion crossing in both elevations. The section shows its short bracket projecting outward, with clearance between the leaves and the glazing. The pane enters a pocket at the head and sits in another at the sill. The standing visitor shares the sill's ground plane. The cream surround holds its outline in both states, and the canyon remains behind the frame when the leaves open the view.

## Balcony edge and stair rail

**Accepted the first painting.** The top tread ends at the landing surface. A vertical post there receives the raked pane on one side and a level pane on the other. The enlarged bend shows the brass rail continuing over that post. The section puts the anchored foot inboard of the outer fascia and the grip above the pane. The visitor's feet meet the landing, and the rail crosses the body near the waist. Changes in viewing angle occur at the edges of the three studies. The round bend is a fixed fitting.

## Furniture and planting

**Accepted the first painting.** The seat spans two feet and the plant stems disappear into soil behind the cream lip. The visitor stands on the same floor as both pieces. Clear space below the banner lets the cloth hang from three loops on the bracketed rod. The picture casts a short wall shadow. Its gold shoulder, dark inner slope and fine lip repeat in the enlarged cut corner. Each object has its own silhouette, including the gap between the visitor and bench.

## Floor and ceiling surfaces

**Accepted the first painting.** Four rows of four tiles make the scale visible. The same joint lines run across the coloured boundary of the cream pad. A broad window reflection crosses several terracotta tiles without obscuring the grout. The ceiling repeats the module with an inner shadow beneath each rim and a round lamp set into the back. The square-on samples have distinct edges and the standing figure sits in a separate margin, clear of the material samples. All three surfaces are fixed.

## Level-zero exhibit hall

**Accepted the first painting.** From walking height the near consoles show a sliver of the turntables behind them. The blue torus and faceted prism rest on separate rings and leave the aisle between them open. Visitors can stand at a panel without blocking that route. Receding floor joints lead to the far glazing, where the mullions cross in front of the canyon and carry the orange hubs. On both sides teal branches meet the cream balcony edges beneath continuous brass rails. Near and far figures bear weight on the same floor, and the foreground stations continue naturally past the framing edges. This is the view for comparing a future engine capture.
