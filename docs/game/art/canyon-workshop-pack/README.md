# Canyon workshop environment pack

The owner selected **B, Canyon workshop**, as the baseline for the valley and
gallery's next wave. This pack develops that direction into views and scenes
for the modeller session. It keeps the painterly, cartoony futuristic treatment
and the gallery's existing roles: exhibits, portal paintings, board games,
stairs, and a lift across four floors.

The [Canyon discovery gallery](../canyon-discovery-pack/README.md) develops the
major demos and player interactions within this selected building language.
Use that pack for the functional room programme and exhibit contents.

## Keep the selected design

The valley is a close-walled caldera, with large warm terracotta and peach rock
strata, green ledges and an open grassy floor. A narrow waterfall, stream,
bridge and rim overlook give it landmarks. Group vegetation and rock planes
into readable painted masses. The [valley brief](../../../plans/valley-and-moons.md)
owns the enclosing scale, clear floor and two cratered moons.

The gallery is an asymmetric stack of thick cream and ochre modules with
rounded rectangular corners and inset turquoise glazing. In B's front view,
the lower terraced wing is on the left, the higher projecting wing on the right,
and the glass lift tower stands near their meeting. Deep teal branched arches
carry the projecting mass. Orange fan-shaped shades pivot on dark circular
hubs. Keep flat planted terraces and terracotta paving.

Use that same material and shape vocabulary indoors. Portal paintings are
framed artworks with visible thickness and clear approaches. The board game
room is a usable room with substantial tables and clear routes. The atrium
connects real floor levels through stairs and lift landings. There is one lift
car travelling in one continuous shaft; the corrected atrium and lift detail
develop that assembly rather than repeating the source painting's ambiguous
stack of occupied capsules.

## Modeller handoff

Start with the selected baseline and overview for the large masses. Establish
one coherent four-floor blockout before adding cladding, windows, shades or
landscape detail. Use the rear view to complete unseen surfaces in that same
blockout. The source image remains the authority if a later painting suggests
a different overall silhouette; individual details must fit the coherent
building rather than determine a second building for each camera.

The images describe form, materials, space and mood. They are perspective
paintings rather than measured elevations. Derive consistent dimensions in
the blockout, using the existing gallery's 4.5 m floor spacing as a starting
scale and the Moth's 2.06 m suited height for doors, routes and furniture.
Keep the four floors, stairs, lift, exhibit routes, portal wing and board room
usable while developing the new exterior.

Use the board room study for furniture and spatial design. The actual game
surfaces and positions come from the existing authored tables.

The existing implementation and functions are mapped by
[Debug world shape gallery](../../../../worlds/debug/shapes.md),
[hall.puck](../../../../worlds/debug/hall.puck),
[paintings.puck](../../../../worlds/debug/paintings.puck) and
[chess.puck](../../../../worlds/debug/chess.puck) and
[go.puck](../../../../worlds/debug/go.puck). The
[SDF authoring skill](../../../../.claude/skills/sdf-authoring/SKILL.md)
owns the primitive and surface vocabulary; the
[Moth pack](../moth-concept-pack/README.md) owns the character's fixed design.
These files are starting systems to develop, not a requirement to preserve the
old rectangular shell's silhouette.

Model large terrain planes, module shells, arches and glazing first. Then add
the shade and lift assemblies, pathways and room furniture. Keep paint and
material borders as surface treatment where they do not change the silhouette.
Use this pack as the reference set for that work; no world implementation is
included in this art wave.
