# The clean measurement window on the RTX 4070

The owner asked for "a truly clean measurement on both machines", to be used to choose the next lanes. This page is the
RTX 4070 half. The RTX 2060 partner took the same set at the same code; the two readings together decide the next
lanes.

Code under test: `e823416192` (release/m1, `dffd89396c` plus a change to the CLI's cache store only), Release, built
before the window. Its mesh bake identity is `57751461d4d285c4b6c69337a1243e53` on Direct3D 12 (the store's own
`.identity` file). The cold-bake comparison also ran the main checkout, whose bake code is efficiency3's (head
`f87cd99f73`; since `8d736239a3` it changed only `WorldReleaseDeploymentStore` and documents), bake identity
`7f6ba2716a289eebb546e17f810d1fd1`.

Machine: RTX 4070 (driver 617.42), 16 logical processors, 32 GB, Windows 10.0.26300. Every windowed World opened at
2560 by 1440 under the `rtx4070` profile (table `high`, device and host memory caps 8192 MiB), with `--devices none`.
The raw reports, frame CSVs, stderr logs and sample traces are in
`.claude/worktrees/lead-bisect/artifacts/window4070/` on the lead machine; they are scratch and are not committed.

The frame bar is read as the benchmark plan says: each iteration's work (its interval less the pacer's wait) against
16.7 ms, in motion, in a run whose machine verdict is clean. Every iteration's interval p50 on Direct3D 12 sits at
16.67 ms (v-sync); the misses are what a player sees as stutter and hitches.

## Conditions and what disturbed the window

Before each leg I read `puck lanes`, `puck lease list` and `puck host load`. No other World, build, test run or lease
holder ran during any leg. The `hotfix` worktree and the main checkout gained commits during the window (the main
checkout moved from `8d736239a3` to `f87cd99f73`), but no process from them ran while I measured: CPU stayed at 1 to
7% between legs and every benchmark run's competition reading is under its bar.

Every `puck benchmark` leg ran under the quiet reservation:

```sh
puck lease quiet --holder window4070 --wait 600 --settle 30 -- puck benchmark --route debug --backend <b> --cache-dir <store> --output <dir>
puck lease quiet --holder window4070 --wait 600 --settle 30 -- puck benchmark --cache-dir <store> --output <dir>   # the standing set
```

| Run | Machine verdict | Other processes | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| r1, cold debug route, Direct3D 12 | **DISTURBED** | 592 CPU s in 215 s (2.8 cores); the named programs add to under 7 s | Unattributed CPU while the empty store filled |
| r2, warm debug route, Direct3D 12 | clean | 0.83 cores | |
| r3, cold debug route, Vulkan | **DISTURBED** | 305 CPU s in 178 s (1.7 cores); named programs under 11 s | As r1 |
| r4, the standing set (debug and moon on both backends, remote, four-seat) | clean on all six runs | 0.41 to 0.85 cores | |

The unattributed load appears only while a store is being written. My inference, not confirmed: it is Windows
Defender scanning the newly written mesh files. `MsMpEng` is a protected process whose CPU time the recorder cannot read
(`Get-Process` returns none for it), and its raw counter rose only 292 CPU seconds over the 45 minutes of the clean
standing set. The two cold runs therefore give a cold-route time, not a frame-bar verdict.

Several chapters carry a chapter-level DISTURBED flag of the form "the simulation stepped no tick for N ms". That is
the engine's own stall, not competition: it recurs at the same places in every run (the first chapter's opening ticks,
the city painting, the lift), so it is reported below as a hitch.

What the columns say:

- **The host bar fails by what the account does not hold.** The host pool holds 1.3 to 2.9 GB of play, yet private
  bytes are 13 to 17 GB. The census' own split at the end of r2's first chapter: managed 3,119 MB (763 MB named),
  runtime-and-driver 2,546 MB, native allocator 1,510 MB (400 MB named); `play-beyond-held` 4,623 MiB; the walk counts
  6.6 GB of driver-mapped private pages (device-local allocations, which cost commit but no RAM).
- **The managed heap grows through every run and never comes back down**: 2.9 to 4.8 GB over r2 (LOH 2.0 to 3.6 GB),

  leak suspect, not a proven leak; the leak method (repeat one action, `host.memory collect` between) decides it.
- **Authoring held** is near zero in play, as it should be; it rises to 1.0 to 1.2 GB where a chapter bakes (the east
  wing).
- **Device**: Direct3D 12 peaks at 7.3 to 7.5 GB in the hall chapters, and one run went to 8,279 MiB in the city

## The largest costs, ranked

Ranked by how far each keeps the RTX 4070 from its bars, and by how much one structural change would recover.

1. **The GPU is the binding limit at 2560 by 1440 on Direct3D 12.** The main view's passes sum to 15 to 16.4 ms a frame
   in the hall chapters at 89 to 91% utilization, so any hitch or extra view misses v-sync; standing frames with 3 to
   4 ms of work still take 33 ms. The cost is spread: `resolve` 2.5 to 3.8, `views` 1.4 to 3.2, `surface` 1.2 to 2.2,
   `views-filter` 1.2 to 1.8, `views-probes` 0.7 to 1.3, `mesh` 0.7 to 1.3 ms. Every session window adds its own passes
   (1.9 ms for one painting in view). Evidence: sections 1, 5 and 8.
2. **The frame thread produces 5.4 ms at p50 on Direct3D 12 and 7.2 ms on Vulkan, and 8 to 15 ms in motion.** Capture
   (`WorldFramePresenter.CaptureFrame`: finish, pack and place of the composed frame), Record and Pump are each 1 to
   4 ms, and all of them scale with the worlds and views the process hosts: hiding the session windows halves produce
   (4.55 to 2.51 ms), and the remote client, which hosts no world, produces at 2.73 ms. Evidence: sections 5, 8 and 10.
3. **Vulkan-only frame-thread and boot costs.** A desktop-capture screen copies its pixels on the frame thread
   (`WorldScreenBinder.CaptureWindow` into `GpuRegion.Write`, about 2 ms an iteration, against nothing on Direct3D 12),
   and Record is 5 to 8 ms an iteration in motion. The warm boot takes 104 s to its first world frame because pipeline
   creation waits behind a lock that `VulkanPipelineCache.Serialize` holds for writing. Evidence: sections 5 and 6.
4. **Memory over the host bar by 1.5 to 2 times, mostly where the account cannot see it.** Working set 8.6 to 11.8 GB,
   private 13 to 17 GB against an 8 GB bar, with only 1.3 to 2.9 GB held by the play pool; a managed heap that grows by
   2 GB over one route; the device bar passed in one Direct3D 12 run (8,279 MiB, 21 over-limit grants). Evidence:
   section 4.
5. **Ticks over budget, and unseen worlds ticking at full rate.** The debug world's tick averages 10 to 14 ms of the
   33 ms tick, and the route sheds 400 to 1,400 ticks a run; the city alone averages 26.6 ms with a p99 of 101.5 ms and a
   899 ms worst, shedding 604 ticks in 20 s. Population (`RunKitPartitions`, `PlanKitStep`) is 45 to 58% of the step.
   The city instance spends 3.5 to 5.2 ms of step a frame while the player walks the hall. Evidence: sections 2 and 5.
6. **Crossing and arrival hitches of 150 to 400 ms**, all on the frame thread's produce: the moon crossing (254 ms,
   Capture 240), the garden and city paintings (Capture or Pump 96 to 272 ms), the lift (Capture 89 to 168 ms), the
   boot's first ticks (Pump 78 to 83 ms with a 71 to 89 ms tick); and on the remote client a GC-heavy hitch storm.
   Evidence: sections 1, 3 and 10.
7. **The cold bake is CPU- and wait-bound at 105 to 120 s per backend** (163 to 179 s to settled), with the GPU busy a
   tenth of the time; efficiency3 removed a third of the counted work without moving the wall time, and its stored
   `valley-overlook` mesh is coarser and shades worse. Evidence: section 9.
8. **The iteration loop around the edit.** A C# restart costs about 31.5 s to the first world frame, 22 s of it the
   boot's unattributed wait before its first frame; a one-colour `.puck` edit shows in 2.3 to 2.8 s but rebuilds every
   session window and once left the meshes stage unsettled; lighting takes 117 to 181 s to settle after a boot.
   Evidence: sections 6 and 7.
