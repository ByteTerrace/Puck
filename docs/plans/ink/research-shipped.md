# Line art in shipped stylized games and offline tools

This memo surveys how shipped stylized games, offline anime renderers, and animated feature pipelines author and render line art. It is the first of three research records for [the ink plan](../ink.md), guided by the rendering decision that [ink is edge signals the engine computes and an ink phase authors write](../../decisions/rendering.md#a-light-is-shadowed-wherever-it-lights-and-cel-shading-is-a-style-per-material).

The short conclusion is that no single edge type or line recipe covers stylized line art. Shipped works span a spectrum from **no dark outlines at all** (*The Wind Waker*, *Breath of the Wild*) through **artist-weighted character hulls with authored inner lines** (*Guilty Gear Xrd*, *Genshin Impact*) and **full-scene screen-space edge filters paired with inked textures** (*Borderlands*, *Sable*, *Hi-Fi Rush*) to **priority-ordered multi-edge vector pipelines with per-stroke tapering and breakup** (*PSOFT Pencil+ 4*, Blender's Freestyle and Grease Pencil Line Art, and Sony Pictures Imageworks' *Kismet*). Every successful real-time game separates *finding where an edge sits* from *deciding whether and how to draw it*, and every one restricts, tints, tapers, or fades lines so distant geometry does not collapse into black noise.

## How this survey handles sources

External techniques below are cited to the papers, GDC talks, vendor manuals, and technical articles retrieved during this round. Where a primary studio talk or official manual was not publicly accessible in full—common for console titles from the 2000s and for proprietary film tools—this memo relies on technical press coverage, conference summaries, or community frame analyses and says so plainly. Where secondary sources contradict each other, both claims are recorded rather than averaged into a false consensus. Every statement about Puck cites the source file and symbol read in this repository.

## Shipped games

### Jet Set Radio

Smilebit's *Jet Set Radio* (Sega Dreamcast, 2000) is the historical baseline for real-time 3D cel outlines. Because sixth-generation hardware like the PowerVR2 lacked programmable post-process pixel shaders, technical retrospectives and NPR overviews report that *Jet Set Radio* drew outlines using a second geometry pass: an expanded, backface-culled shell (the inverted hull) rendered in solid black behind selected characters and props ([Real-Time Rendering / NPR overview](https://www.realtimerendering.com/), secondary technical retrospectives).

- **Edges inked:** Outer silhouettes and convex overlaps of opted-in meshes. Material boundaries, planar intersections, and shadow terminators do not ink unless modeled as separate shells.
- **Authoring:** Uniform black shell per opted-in model, controlled at asset setup by programmers and modelers rather than per-pixel artists.
- **Motion stability:** Geometrically locked to the mesh so lines never flicker from screen-space sampling, though raw world-space extrusion thins with distance and can split across sharp normal seams.
- **Cost:** Doubles vertex processing and draw submission for outlined meshes; no Dreamcast frame-time breakdown was found.

### The Wind Waker

*The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker* (Nintendo GameCube, 2002) is frequently cited in online cel-shading tutorials as an inverted-hull outline game, yet primary observations of the shipped game contradict that claim. Generic shader tutorials and summaries attribute black backface-extruded hulls to Link and world props, whereas player and press analyses of the actual GameCube and Wii U releases point out that *The Wind Waker* uses very few or no stark black outlines on its characters and environments in motion, relying instead on hard two-tone TEV lighting ramps (*ZAtoon*), crisp painted textures, and high-contrast silhouettes against bright backgrounds. While the [`zeldaret/tww`](https://github.com/zeldaret/tww) decompilation confirms Nintendo's J3D display-list and TEV combiner architecture, this round did not open a J3D draw-packet source file proving whether specific props or effects use an inverted-hull pass; any claim that *The Wind Waker* uses global character outlines should be treated as unverified secondary mythology.

The design lesson for Puck holds either way: **stylized cel shading does not require dark outlines on every object**. An ink system must let authors opt materials out cleanly, or reserve ink for selective accents, without coupling outlines to banded lighting.

### Okami

Clover Studio's *Okami* (PlayStation 2, 2006) targeted traditional Japanese *sumi-e* ink wash and *ukiyo-e* woodblock prints. No primary Clover Studio GDC or CEDEC graphics paper was retrieved in this round; secondary technical breakdowns and emulator-era analyses describe a three-part look:

- **Edges inked:** Outer silhouettes on characters, enemies, and major environment props via an inverted-hull backface pass; interior brush strokes, fur, and folds painted directly into diffuse textures; and dynamic player-drawn strokes rendered as tapered 2D/3D procedural splines during the Celestial Brush pause screen.
- **Authoring:** Bold black hulls modulated by hand-authored textures and vertex weighting to fake brush tapering on fine features like Amaterasu's tail, unified across the frame by a subtle screen-space *washi* paper-grain overlay.
- **Motion stability:** Geometry hulls stay stable during motion, though sharp mesh intersections can reveal hull tearing or clipping. The stationary or gently scrolling paper texture introduces a deliberate canvas-grain feel rather than world-locked breakup.
- **Cost:** Extra geometry pass for outlined meshes on PS2 hardware plus a full-screen paper overlay; no measured frame timings were found.

### Borderlands

Gearbox Software's *Borderlands* series (Unreal Engine) pairs heavily hand-inked diffuse textures with a full-screen post-process edge filter. Technical breakdowns of Gearbox's Unreal pipeline describe a screen-space Sobel (or 4- to 5-tap cross) operator evaluated over the scene depth buffer and world-normal G-buffer rather than final color, avoiding false edges from lighting and specular highlights.

- **Edges inked:** Depth discontinuities (outer silhouettes and overlapping object boundaries) and world-normal discontinuities (hard creases, armor seams, weapon panel lines). Interior crosshatching, ink washes, and dark contact accents are hand-painted directly into the albedo maps by texture artists.
- **Authoring:** Technical artists tune global or per-zone post-process thresholds, stroke thickness (in screen texels), and linear-depth distance fade so distant terrain does not turn solid black. Individual surface character comes from texture artists painting ink lines into the diffuse map.
- **Motion stability:** Constant screen-space width keeps close and mid-range silhouettes readable without duplicating geometry, though naive screen-space filters struggle with transparent objects (which do not write standard depth) and can shimmer on sub-pixel distant geometry when distance fade is too weak.
- **Cost:** Independent of scene triangle count (zero extra draw calls), bounded by a single full-screen pass of 4 to 9 depth and normal taps per pixel. No Gearbox millisecond measurement was found in retrieved sources.

### Guilty Gear Xrd, Dragon Ball FighterZ, and Guilty Gear Strive

Arc System Works' GDC 2015 presentation by Junya Christopher Motomura ([GuiltyGearXrd's Art Style: The X Factor Between 2D and 3D](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022031/GuiltyGearXrd-s-Art-Style-The)) remains the clearest published blueprint for artist-directed real-time anime line art. Arc System Works rejected post-process outlines because uniform screen-space filters broke down in dramatic close-ups and lacked artist-painted line weight.

- **Edges inked:** Outer silhouettes and major creases via a modified inverted hull; interior anatomical, facial, and clothing lines via authored **inner line maps** packed into the alpha channel of each character's Illumination/Material (ILM) texture.
- **Authoring:** Character modelers paint four per-vertex control channels in the mesh's vertex color to direct both shading and outlines. In slide transcriptions of Motomura's GDC 2015 talk, the four channels are assigned as: **R** for shading threshold offset, **G** for camera-distance outline expansion scaling, **B** for outline Z-offset (pushing outlines forward or back to resolve overlaps and internal creases), and **A** for per-vertex line thickness. Secondary Reddit summaries occasionally conflate G and A (describing the green channel as thickness); the GDC slide breakdown puts thickness in alpha and distance scaling in green. Outline color is not flat black—it is tinted from the underlying material and lighting context. To prevent jagged diagonal stairstepping on texture-drawn inner lines, artists draw inner lines strictly horizontally and vertically in the ILM texture and intentionally warp the character's UVs along curves so bilinear filtering and mipmapping smooth cleanly along a single axis.
- **Evolution in *Dragon Ball FighterZ* and *Guilty Gear Strive*:** Secondary technical analyses note that later Arc System Works titles kept the same inverted-hull plus warped-UV inner-line foundation while automating normal editing for heavier cloth/hair physics and tightening per-vertex visibility masks so facial lines can disappear cleanly at certain camera angles.
- **Motion stability:** Exceptionally high. Hulls stay welded to the deforming mesh with camera-distance compensation, and UV-aligned inner lines remain razor-sharp across zoom levels without screen-space crawl.
- **Cost:** Doubles character geometry passes for the inverted hull, consumes all four vertex-color channels plus an ILM texture alpha channel, and carries heavy per-asset manual authoring cost (modelers edit normals, vertex colors, and warped UVs per character). No GPU frame-time breakdown was retrieved.

### Breath of the Wild and Genshin Impact

Comparing *The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild* (Nintendo, 2017) with *Genshin Impact* (miHoYo / HoYoverse, 2020) shows two opposite choices for open-world stylized characters.

**Breath of the Wild.** Secondary tutorials frequently lump *Breath of the Wild* in with inverted-hull outline games, yet closer visual and Cemu/RenderDoc community analyses contradict that claim: *Breath of the Wild* **does not use dark black outlines** around Link or its environments. Instead, Nintendo paired a PBR-lit painterly terrain with deferred-stenciled toon characters whose silhouettes separate from the background via **directional-light-coupled Fresnel rim lighting**—a bright, stepped highlight along grazing angles that responds to the sun's position and attenuates in shadow. This reinforces the same architectural lesson as *The Wind Waker*: silhouette emphasis can be *bright* (light-driven rim) as well as *dark* (ink), and characters and terrain often want completely different edge treatments in the same frame.

**Genshin Impact.** Community shader reconstructions and Unity technical breakdowns of *Genshin Impact* show an evolved Arc System Works-style character hull adapted for an open world:

- **Edges inked:** Character silhouettes and selected internal contours via an inverted-hull pass; environments generally omit heavy character hulls.
- **Authoring:** To solve the classic inverted-hull defect where split normals at hard seams tear open gaps in the outline, technical artists pre-bake **smoothed (averaged) normals** into a vertex attribute (commonly vertex color RGB or a UV channel) and extrude along those smoothed normals. Vertex-color masks taper width smoothly to zero on delicate tips (such as hair strands) and suppress lines in cluttered folds. Camera-distance scaling keeps width legible from close-ups to open-world exploration, and outline color is dynamically tinted from the underlying material's albedo (dark brown on gold, deep hair hue on hair) rather than pure black.
- **Motion stability:** Very stable on animated characters, with smooth seams and controlled distance scaling.
- **Cost:** A second character draw pass and extra vertex-stream storage for baked smoothed normals; no official miHoYo GPU timing was retrieved.

### Sable

Shedworks' *Sable* (2021, built in Unity by Daniel Fineberg and Gregorios Kythreotis) translates Jean Giraud's (Moebius) *ligne claire* ("clear line") comic art into a sunlit 3D open world. Shedworks has not published a full shader code listing; interviews and technical breakdowns report a custom HLSL post-processing pipeline rather than inverted hulls.

- **Edges inked:** Depth discontinuities (silhouettes and overlapping forms) and world-normal discontinuities (structural creases, architecture, rock facets, and fabric folds) detected in screen space, supplemented by hand-drawn decals and surface textures for fine internal linework that edge filters miss.
- **Authoring:** Paired with a flat, single-band shadow threshold and strict time-of-day color palettes so form reads through silhouette and clean line rather than soft gradients. Thresholds and distance attenuation are carefully tuned per element and across desert distances so distant ruins stay legible without collapsing into dark scribbles.
- **Motion stability:** Clean across flat-shaded architecture and dunes when distance thresholds are well tuned, though screen-space normal/depth filters remain sensitive to dense distant geometry and grazing slopes.
- **Cost:** Full-screen custom post-process pass in Unity; no GPU millisecond figure was published.

### Hi-Fi Rush

Tango Gameworks' GDC 2024 presentation by Kosuke Tanaka and Takashi Komada ([3D Toon Rendering in 'Hi-Fi RUSH'](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1034633/3D-Toon-Rendering-in-Hi-Fi-RUSH)) detailed a customized Unreal Engine 4 **deferred toon renderer** targeting native resolution at 60 FPS across characters and environments alike.

- **Edges inked:** Silhouettes and surface creases via a dedicated **Post Process Outline** pass integrated with G-buffer and depth data, plus stylized **Comic Shader** patterns that turn lighting transitions into graphic ink language: procedural 2D signed-distance **halftone dots** in lit areas and rotated **hatching lines** replacing soft SSAO and indirect-lighting gradients in shadowed zones.
- **Authoring:** Level artists place deferred **post-process volumes** across the world to tune toon parameters, shadow steps, colored vs. black shadows, and comic-shader accents locally per area. Hard-edged **decal lights** give artists crisp illumination boundaries instead of smooth falloffs.
- **Motion stability:** Procedural SDF halftone dots and hatching lines are anti-aliased with `smoothstep` and alpha blending alongside customized Temporal AA and toon motion blur to prevent crawl at 60 FPS.
- **Cost:** Built to hold locked 60 FPS at native resolution across whole levels (characters, props, and architecture); the GDC summary retrieved did not include an isolated millisecond cost for the outline pass alone.

## Offline line-art tools and film pipelines

Offline renderers and film studios pay far more compute per frame than a 60 FPS game can afford, but their **authoring vocabularies** show exactly what artists ask for once hardware stops dictating a single uniform black line.

### PSOFT Pencil+ 4

PSOFT's *Pencil+ 4 Line* is a staple of Japanese commercial 3D anime production (3ds Max, Maya, Blender) and also ships as a real-time Unity plugin ([Pencil+ 4 Line documentation](https://docs.psoft.co.jp/pcl400w/en/latest/panel/line_PencilLine/), [Brush Settings](https://docs.psoft.co.jp/pcl400w/en/latest/panel/line_BrushSetting/), [Unity Line List](https://docs.psoft.co.jp/pus400w/jp/latest/panel/line-list/)). Its authoring model centers on two concepts that game shaders rarely expose:

- **Priority-ordered Line Sets:** Artists group objects and materials into an ordered stack of **Line Sets**. A Line Set higher in the list takes precedence over one below it—so a character's eye or skin material can override, recolor, or completely suppress an edge that the character's default object Line Set would otherwise draw.
- **Rich edge taxonomy:** Each Line Set independently toggles **Outline (Object)**, **Open Edge**, **Object Intersection** (where two separate meshes penetrate each other), **Smoothing Boundary** (hard/soft edge transitions), **Material ID Boundary** (where two materials meet on one mesh), and **Normal Angle** (within an explicit min–max angle range), with separate styling for **Visible Lines** and **Hidden Lines** (lines behind occluders).
- **Brush and reduction controls:** Artists author brush textures, organic **Distortion** (waviness/irregularity), **Size Reduction** and **Alpha Reduction** by distance from the camera or a reference object, and **Stroke Size / Alpha Reduction** along each stroke to produce calligraphic entry/exit tapering (*iri-nuki*).
- **Motion stability and cost:** Distance reduction and curve-driven brush settings prevent distant clutter, though underlying geometry-edge visibility can still pop at grazing silhouette angles. No GPU timing benchmarks for the Unity plugin were published in the retrieved docs.

### Blender Freestyle and Grease Pencil Line Art

Blender ships two distinct non-photorealistic line systems, both documented in the official Blender manual ([Freestyle Line Sets](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/freestyle/parameter_editor/line_set.html), [Freestyle Line Styles](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/freestyle/parameter_editor/line_style/index.html), and [Grease Pencil Line Art Modifier](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/grease_pencil/modifiers/generate/line_art.html)).

**Freestyle** implements the programmable NPR line-drawing architecture of Grabli, Durand, Turquin, and Sillion ([ACM TOG 2010](https://doi.org/10.1145/1731047.1731056)) together with DeCarlo, Finkelstein, Rusinkiewicz, and Santella's **Suggestive Contours** ([SIGGRAPH 2003](https://gfx.cs.princeton.edu/pubs/DeCarlo_2003_SCF/)):

- **Edges inked:** Silhouette, External Contour (outer silhouette across overlapping objects), Border (open mesh edges), Crease (dihedral angle threshold), Ridge & Valley (convex peaks and concave troughs on smooth surfaces), Suggestive Contour (almost-silhouettes where a small viewpoint shift would produce a contour), Material Boundary, and artist-flagged Edge Marks and Face Marks, combinable with logical AND/OR predicates.
- **Authoring:** Extracted edges are chained into strokes and shaped by modular **Color**, **Alpha**, and **Thickness** modifiers driven by **Along Stroke** (taper/fade from start to end), **Curvature 3D**, **Crease Angle**, **Distance from Camera / Object**, **Material**, **Noise**, and **Tangent** (calligraphic pen-angle thickness).
- **Motion stability and cost:** Freestyle builds a CPU view map per frame with no built-in temporal coherence. Sub-pixel camera moves or coarse tessellation cause edges to jump between triangles and strokes to re-chain, creating flicker. Render time scales steeply with mesh subdivision and sensitive crease thresholds, making it strictly offline.

**Grease Pencil Line Art** converts 3D scene edges directly into Grease Pencil strokes:

- **Edges inked:** Contour, Crease, Material Borders, **Intersection** (mesh-to-mesh penetration), **Light Contour** (silhouette relative to a light object, giving a geometric shadow-terminator line), Edge Marks, and Loose edges, with explicit **Occlusion Levels** (visible only, or specific hidden-layer depths for cutaway/blueprint looks).
- **Authoring:** Strokes inherit Grease Pencil materials and stackable modifiers (thickness curves, noise, opacity, color) and can be baked to keyframes for manual cleanup.
- **Motion stability and cost:** Faster than Freestyle for interactive previews, yet still recalculates topology per frame. Camera-frustum clipping can change stroke topology when meshes cross the frame border (artists work around this by rendering with 5%–10% overscan), and coplanar faces cause Z-fighting flicker. Dense scenes require collection filtering and distance culling.

### Film pipelines: Spider-Verse and Arcane

Feature animation studios expose the ceiling of expressive line art—and also clarify where real-time procedural ink stops.

**Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse and Across the Spider-Verse (Sony Pictures Imageworks).** As reported in SIGGRAPH 2019/2023 coverage (*fxguide*, *befores & afters*), Sony Pictures Imageworks found that rule-bound toon shaders looked stiff because human comic inkers vary weight, drop lines selectively, and place facial ink by expression rather than raw curvature. For *Into the Spider-Verse*, the studio trained **machine-learning models** on artist drawings to predict how facial ink curves should sit and deform across 3D expressions without swimming. For *Across the Spider-Verse*, FX Supervisor Pav Grochola and his team built **Kismet** (in Houdini) and **Superdraw** to author and procedurally generate **3D ink curves** directly on geometry across radically different universe styles (from watercolor ink bleeds to loose sketchy overshoots extending past silhouettes). Crucially, *Kismet* controlled **temporal redrawing**: lines deliberately tapered, faded, or redrew on twos, fours, or sixes to match hand-drawn comic pacing while staying anchored in 3D space across stereo cameras and rapid character motion.

**Arcane (Fortiche Production / Riot Games).** Studio interviews and technical breakdowns show that *Arcane* rejects uniform global outlines altogether. Artists paint freehand, slightly irregular brushwork directly onto 3D character textures and matte-projected environments (in *Mari* and *3DCoat*), then rely on **Nuke compositing** over 3D utility passes (normals, object position, world position) to blend lighting, selective line accents, and **2D hand-animated FX** animated on twos. The lesson mirrors *The Wind Waker* and *Breath of the Wild* from a painterly angle: subtle, selective, lighting-aware edges integrated with textured brushwork often read as richer than a heavy outline stamped around every polygon.

## Weighing Puck-native combinations against borrowed techniques

Before listing capabilities for Puck's ink phase, it helps to ground the comparison in what Puck already computes every frame and where its architecture differs from standard polygon engines.

### What Puck has today

- **A visibility buffer with exact IDs and per-pixel curvature:** `src/Puck.SdfVm/Assets/Shaders/Sdf/frame/sdf-visibility.hlsli` stores an 8-word visibility record per render-grid pixel: ray parameter `t` (`SdfVisibilityTWord`), object/draw identity (`SdfVisibilityIdentityWord`, decoded via `sdfVisibilityKind` and `sdfVisibilitySource`), material index (`SdfVisibilityMaterialWord`), triangle index (`SdfVisibilityTriangleWord`), dynamic frame slot (`SdfVisibilityFrameSlotWord`), 16-bit octahedral geometric normal (`SdfVisibilityNormalWord`), packed half-float mean curvature and ambient occlusion (`SdfVisibilityShadingWord`), and four shadow visibilities (`SdfVisibilityShadowWord`). Mean curvature is already derived per pixel from the baked triangle's interpolated outward vertex normals in `sdfMeshMeanCurvature` (`src/Puck.SdfVm/Assets/Shaders/Sdf/frame/sdf-mesh.hlsli`) with zero neighbour taps and zero field queries.
- **An SDF-to-mesh bake that already knows creases and material seams:** `SdfDualContouring`, `SdfMeshPlanarMerge`, and `SdfMeshCollapse` (documented in [rendering decisions](../../decisions/rendering.md#a-raster-pass-draws-only-what-it-can-show)) preserve crease edges and material boundaries explicitly as geometric lines during LOD collapse, keeping separate vertices per crease side and locking corners.
- **A bounded, tile-binned authored shade program system:** `SdfShadeOps.Table` and `SdfShadeOps.Inputs` (`src/Puck.SignedDistance/Shading/SdfShadeOps.cs`) define a static-cost, per-pixel presentation IR with `surface`, `light`, and `compose` phases, compiled by `SdfShadeProgram.Compile`, interpreted immediately, specialized in the background (`SdfShadeHlsl.Specialize`), and dispatched by 8×8 tile bins (`sdf-world-views-filter.comp.hlsl`, `sdf-world-views-bins.comp.hlsl`). Authors already have world- and object-space coordinates (`surface.position`, `surface.layerPosition` riding the winning dynamic slot), pixel footprint (`surface.footprint`), curvature (`surface.curvature`), glass contact (`surface.contact`, computed by `sdfGlassContact` in `src/Puck.SdfVm/Assets/Shaders/Sdf/passes/sdf-glass-stage.hlsli`), procedural 3D noise (`lattice`, `latticeGradient`, `hash`), procedural antialiased lines (`lines` via `sdfLineCoverage` in `src/Puck.SdfVm/Assets/Shaders/Sdf/shade/shade-weathering.hlsli`), 3D coverage-distance primitives (`shape` via `sdfShadeShape`), and live state-driven parameters (`lanes.slot0..3`, `lanes.world0..3`).
- **A fixed, narrow legacy outline:** `src/Puck.SdfVm/Assets/Shaders/Sdf/shade/sdf-toon-outline.hlsli` (`sdfToonOutline`, `sdfToonEdge`) runs inside `src/Puck.SdfVm/Assets/Shaders/Sdf/passes/sdf-light-stage.hlsli` right after `sdfShadeComposePhase`, sampling up to 16 neighbour visibility records per styled pixel for tangent-plane depth jumps and normal creases only. It ignores `SdfVisibilityIdentityWord` and `SdfVisibilityMaterialWord`, cannot see light terminators or glass (`sdf-glass-stage.hlsli` never calls it), hardcodes `step(s.t, t)` so the nearer surface always wins, and multiplies scene light by a flat tint (`lerp(1.0, style.ink, ...)`).
- **Nested views, portals, and dynamic resolution:** Portals (`SdfScreenFace.Opening`) and camera screens render as separate `sdf.world` render-graph instances (`WorldViewInstances`, `WorldWindowFrustumFit` in [motion and views](../../rendering/sdf/handbook/motion-and-views.md)), each with its own visibility buffer, footprint quantization (`RenderGraphExtent.Quantize`), and dynamic render grid (`SdfViewSnapshot.ResolvedRenderScale`). `sdf-toon-outline.hlsli` already enforces that a screen or portal face does not ink over the already-shaded image inside it, while `sdf-light-stage.hlsli` runs outlines before temporal resolve (`sdf-resolve.comp.hlsl`), atmosphere (`sdf-composite.comp.hlsl`), and display tone/bloom (`SdfWorldPackage.WithDisplay`).

### Why Puck should combine rather than copy

1. **Do not import the inverted-hull pass as the primary mechanism.** *Guilty Gear Xrd* and *Genshin Impact* prove that artists love per-part width control, tapering, and seam-free silhouettes—not backface shells per se. Inverted hulls double draw counts, require baked smoothed normals on split seams, cannot detect object intersections or material boundaries on one surface, and fight Puck's baked SDF mesh pipeline (where `SdfMeshCollapse` deliberately splits crease normals and rigid creations share one immutable mesh across copies). Instead, **compute edge signals and distance-to-edge once from Puck's visibility buffer**, and give authors the *control* that inverted-hull artists value (per-material width, distance curves, tapering, color tinting, and suppression) inside the authored `ink` phase.
2. **Pair visibility-buffer edges with `surface.curvature` and light-response signals.** Pure depth/normal Sobel filters (*Borderlands*, *Sable*) miss gentle curved ridges, suggestive-contour cues, and shadow terminators. Puck already writes world-unit mean curvature into `SdfSurfaceSample.curvature` at zero extra bandwidth cost, and its `light` and `compose` phases already evaluate lighting cosines and shadow visibility (`worlds/standard/shading.puck`'s `cel`). Exposing edge-pass signals (depth jump, normal crease, object/draw ID boundary, material ID boundary, signed/unsigned distance to the nearest edge) *alongside* `surface.curvature` and lighting/shadow inputs lets one authored `ink` phase express *Borderlands* creases, *Freestyle* ridge/valley and curvature accents, *Hi-Fi Rush* shadow-band hatching/halftones, and *Breath of the Wild* light-coupled rim lines—without offline view-map raycasting.
3. **Replace UV-warped inner line textures with Puck's layer-space SDF `shape`, `lines`, and `lattice` ops.** *Guilty Gear Xrd* warped UVs because polygon meshes rely on 2D textures. Puck's baked SDF creations do not carry artist-unwrapped UVs, yet `SdfShadeOps` already evaluates analytical 3D `shape` primitives (`sdfShadeShape`), triplanar antialiased `lines` (`sdfLineCoverage`), and 3D `lattice` noise in `surface.layerPosition` (which rides the moving creation slot stably) with `surface.footprint` providing exact analytical anti-aliasing and frequency fade (already demonstrated by `weathered` and `flow` in `worlds/standard/shading.puck`). Anchoring ink breakup, dry-brush texture, wobble, and authored inner lines to `surface.layerPosition` and `surface.footprint` gives world-locked strokes that do not swim in motion and need no UVs.
4. **Bring Pencil+ 4's boundary precedence into material data.** At a shared boundary between two materials or two objects (hair over face, trim over stone, water meeting shore, cel character against uninked terrain), *Pencil+ 4* shows that "nearest depth always wins" is too rigid: an uninked foreground leaf or skin highlight may need to yield to or suppress an adjacent ink line, and two touching materials on the same mesh have identical depth. Carrying an authored **ink priority / boundary-ownership rule** per material lets the engine's edge pass decide unambiguously which side owns the boundary and which material's `ink` phase shades the stroke.
5. **Handle glass, water, and portals natively.** Because `sdf-glass-stage.hlsli` already computes `sdfGlassContact` (distance from the glass/water surface to the opaque surface behind it) and `surface.thickness`, glass and water can ink both their silhouettes/creases and their **shoreline / contact intersection lines**—reproducing *Pencil+ 4* and *Grease Pencil* intersection lines for water and transparent shells without needing an opaque depth write. Across **portals** (`SdfScreenFace.Opening`) and **dynamic resolution** (`SdfViewSnapshot.ResolvedRenderScale`), expressing line width in **display pixels** (decoupled from the internal render grid) or **world metres** (scaled through `surface.footprint`) keeps stroke weight continuous between a portal's frame and the world seen through it, even when the portal view renders at a smaller quantized extent.
6. **Know what stays outside a per-pixel ink phase.** Two film-pipeline capabilities do not reduce cleanly to a pixel-local ink phase over a visibility-buffer edge field: (a) **overshoot strokes that extend into empty sky beyond a silhouette** (*Spider-Verse*'s *Kismet* sketchy extensions) unless the engine's edge distance field propagates a few pixels outward onto background pixels, and (b) **stroke-length parameterization** (Blender Freestyle and *Pencil+ 4*'s *Along Stroke* taper from start vertex to end vertex of a chained polyline), because a visibility-buffer edge pass sees local edge distance and tangent rather than a globally chained polyline's endpoints. Local tapers driven by edge strength, crease angle, curvature, view angle, light cosine, and world-space noise cover most real-time game looks; true start-to-end polyline chaining or silhouette-extending 3D curves would require an explicit geometry/curve pass if ever wanted.

## Verification notes and gaps

This survey verified canonical primary documentation and papers for *Guilty Gear Xrd* (GDC 2015), *Hi-Fi Rush* (GDC 2024), *PSOFT Pencil+ 4* (3ds Max and Unity manuals), Blender's Freestyle and Grease Pencil Line Art (official Blender manual), DeCarlo et al. (2003), and Grabli et al. (2010). Details for *Jet Set Radio*, *The Wind Waker*, *Okami*, *Borderlands*, *Breath of the Wild*, *Genshin Impact*, *Sable*, *Spider-Verse*, and *Arcane* were gathered through web search summaries of conference coverage, studio interviews (*fxguide*, *befores & afters*, *80.lv*, *Cook and Becker*, *Fortiche*), and community shader/emulator analyses rather than direct source-code inspection. Two concrete gaps remain for the GPU and field lanes rather than this survey:

- **No shipped game published an isolated GPU millisecond budget for outlines.** Real-time cost choices in Puck must be measured directly on the bar devices rather than extrapolated from game post-mortems.
- **Secondary tutorials routinely misattribute black outlines to cel-shaded Nintendo titles.** Both *The Wind Waker* and *Breath of the Wild* are widely cited online as inverted-hull outline games despite primary visual evidence to the contrary; where a game has no official graphics talk, community tutorials cannot be taken at face value.

## Capabilities an author-facing ink phase must express

To cover the looks surveyed above—from subtle *Breath of the Wild* and *Arcane* accents through *Guilty Gear*, *Genshin Impact*, *Borderlands*, *Sable*, and *Hi-Fi Rush* to the real-time subset of *Pencil+ 4* and *Freestyle*—an author-facing `ink` phase (backed by engine-computed edge signals and tile classification so uninked pixels pay nothing) must let authors express the following:

1. **Per-edge-class selection and independent weighting.** Enable, threshold, or weight each edge class separately rather than collapsing them into one scalar:
   - **Depth silhouette / occlusion jump** (tested against the surface's tangent plane so sloping planes do not self-ink, and distinguishing sky/background silhouettes from foreground-over-background overlaps).
   - **Normal crease** (continuous dihedral angle or normal dot-product response, with separate sensitivity for convex vs. concave creases where desired).
   - **Object / draw boundary** (distinct `SdfVisibilityIdentityWord` draw sources meeting at similar depth, catching touching or intersecting objects).
   - **Material boundary** (distinct `SdfVisibilityMaterialWord` slots meeting on the same draw, matching *Pencil+ 4* and *Freestyle* Material Boundaries).
   - **Curvature ridges and valleys** (reading `surface.curvature` directly—positive for convex ridges, negative for concave valleys—so smooth sculpted SDF forms can carry suggestive-contour-like internal lines without hard mesh creases).
   - **Light and shadow boundaries** (reading post-lighting signals—such as key-light cosine, shadow visibility, or ambient occlusion—so authors can ink cel terminators, drive *Hi-Fi Rush* lit halftones and shadowed hatching, or draw *Breath of the Wild* light-coupled bright rims).
   - **Contact and intersection edges on glass and water** (reading `surface.contact` and glass/opaque boundaries so water shorelines and transparent rims can ink without breaking refraction).
   - **Authored inner lines** (composing `shape`, `lines`, or layer-space procedural patterns with engine edges in the same phase, replacing UV-mapped inner line textures).
2. **Distance-to-edge coverage for smooth variable width.** Read the distance (in display pixels, render pixels, or world metres via `surface.footprint`) from the pixel to the nearest detected edge—not just a binary hit—so an author's `ink` phase can compute arbitrary widths, soft or crisp anti-aliased edges, double strokes, or drop-off profiles without re-tapping neighbour pixels inside the shade program.
3. **Flexible width laws (screen-space, world-space, and hybrid distance curves).** Express stroke width as a function of camera distance, `surface.footprint`, view angle (grazing angle), edge strength (crease angle or depth jump), curvature, and lighting—covering constant display-pixel *ligne claire* (*Sable*, *Borderlands*), clamped camera-distance scaling (*Guilty Gear*, *Genshin Impact*, *Pencil+ 4* Size Reduction), and world-metre strokes that remain physically consistent through portals.
4. **Local tapering and selective suppression.** Taper width or opacity down to zero by curvature, crease sharpness, grazing angle, distance, lighting (such as thinning lines on brightly lit edges and thickening them in shadow, a classic comic inking rule), or per-instance state lanes (`lanes.slot0..3`, `lanes.world0..3`), and opt materials completely out of inking (essential for foliage, distant terrain, sky, screens, and *Wind Waker* / *Breath of the Wild* looks).
5. **Expressive ink colour and compositing mode.** Compute ink color in linear scene light from the lit color, base `surface.albedo` (for *Genshin* / *Guilty Gear* hue-tinted outlines), lighting or shadow tint, or a fixed hue, and choose how it combines with the shaded pixel: **multiply/darken** (classic dark ink), **alpha-over replace** (opaque colored line, white technical line, or comic ink), or **additive / lighten** (sun-lit rim lines, glowing energy edges, *Breath of the Wild* highlights).
6. **World-locked breakup, wobble, and texture.** Perturb edge distance or modulate opacity and width using 3D procedural ops (`lattice`, `latticeGradient`, `hash`, `lines`) anchored to `surface.layerPosition` and band-limited by `surface.footprint` so dry-brush gaps, painterly roughness, and hand-drawn edge irregularity stick cleanly to moving creations instead of swimming across the screen—plus optional stepped-clock (`clock` quantized to 2s or 4s) modulation for authors who want deliberate *Spider-Verse*-style line boil.
7. **Boundary ownership and priority between neighbouring materials.** Declare per-material precedence (and whether a material can ink onto a neighbour or inhibit a neighbour's ink across a shared seam) so the engine's edge pass resolves ownership at depth, object, and material boundaries cleanly instead of hardcoding "nearest pixel always wins."
8. **Edge direction (screen tangent) for calligraphic and hatching styles.** Expose the local edge normal or tangent direction from the edge pass so authors can vary width by stroke orientation (calligraphic nib angle, matching *Freestyle*'s Tangent modifier) or align crosshatching and brush grain along or across an edge.
9. **Clean boundaries with screens, portals, atmosphere, and temporal resolve.** Keep screens and portal openings (`SdfScreenFace.Opening`) from re-inking their already-inked interior pictures while allowing the portal's own frame to ink; run ink in linear scene light before atmosphere (`sdf-composite.comp.hlsl`) so distant lines naturally recede into fog and haze; keep width invariant to `SdfViewSnapshot.ResolvedRenderScale` so dynamic resolution never thickens or pops lines; and cooperate with temporal reprojection (`sdf-resolve.comp.hlsl`) so sharp 1–2 pixel strokes do not ghost or blur in motion.
10. **Zero-cost skipping where no edge is near.** Keep the `ink` phase strictly separated from bulk surface shading (just as Puck already bins tiles with `sdf-world-views-bins.comp.hlsl`) so pixels and 8×8 tiles with no nearby edge and no interior ink rule never pay the ink phase's registers or instructions.
