# GPU edges and authored ink

Find the boundaries once in each view that asks for ink. Keep the two surfaces that meet at a boundary, its position in the image and the reason it exists. Then an author can draw a thin crease on a face, a wide silhouette on a coat and no line where two panels meet, all from the same detection pass. For ordinary widths I would spend the GPU budget on finding the right boundary within a few pixels. Flooding a distance field across the whole image solves a much larger problem. ## What can move out of views

The current outline already solves an important problem. Reconstruct the neighbour's position from its camera ray and hit distance, then measure how far that point lies off the centre surface's tangent plane, in pixel footprints. A steep flat floor can have a large change in ray distance and still give zero plane error. The near side owns a depth jump. A separate cosine comparison catches normal creases and fades out when the two distances separate. A miss beyond the surface counts as a silhouette, and a tap outside the view contributes nothing. The shader samples eight directions at one integer radius, or at two radii for widths above one, and blends their edge responses. The width starts in output pixels, scales to the render grid and falls with distance. These are samples on two rings. A thin feature between them can be missed. `sdfToonEdge` and `sdfToonOutline` (since replaced by the ink phase's edge search in `passes/sdf-views-filter.hlsli`) · [sdfToonStyle](../../../src/Puck.SdfVm/Assets/Shaders/Sdf/shade/sdf-toon.hlsli)

The actual caller is inside the lighting stage. After the authored compose phase, a styled surface multiplies its scene light by the ink tint under outline coverage. The editor grid follows. The enclosing views stage then applies air transmittance. A miss, a sampled screen and the screen-material fallback bypass the material outline. Extracting the neighbour reads is straightforward. Preserving that order for arbitrary authored ink colour, glass and a moving portal is the real change. It gives ray distance, kind and source, material, triangle, transform slot, packed normal, curvature and ambient occlusion, and four shadow visibilities. For geometry edges, load only the words needed and share the tile's border samples. The existing views filter shows how to gather an apron before a group barrier. The surface pass already gets the visible mesh's interpolated normal and curvature. This makes curvature available for line weight without neighbouring field evaluations. ## Which boundaries to keep

Earlier GPU work extracted silhouettes and creases from depth and normals, texture boundaries from region IDs, and cast-shadow boundaries from a per-light visibility signal. Each buffer answers a different question. Hertzmann's folded-paper example shows why one threshold on depth or normals fails on some views. The following are proposed inputs for Puck's ink program. [ATI course notes, Image-Space Outlining](https://www.pixelmaven.com/jason/articles/ATI/ATIHardwareShading_2002_Chapter3-1.pdf) · [Hertzmann, Outlines with Edge Detection](https://mrl.cs.nyu.edu/publications/npr-course1999/hertzmann-intro3d.pdf)

| Signal | Make a candidate when | What the artist gains |
|---|---|---|
| Silhouette or depth break | One sample hits and its neighbour misses, or their reconstructed points separate across the nearer tangent plane. Divide plane error by the local projected pixel footprint. Keep side, orientation and continuous strength. | A contour survives coplanar slopes, with a known foreground owner at an occlusion. |
| Crease | Two nearby hits have a large angular change in geometric normal. Carry the cosine and plane residual separately. | A fold on one material can ink. Authored ripples and normal-map detail can stay out of the geometric crease signal. |
| Object, instance, material | Point-load integer labels on both sides. Compare a logical part or object key, a placement key and the resolved material independently. | Two copies of the same creation can separate even when their palette matches. Adjacent materials on a single flat part can select either ink or silence. |
| Luminance | Compare uninked, linear scene luminance across neighbouring samples, for example a difference of log luminances with a floor near black. Store strength and gradient direction. Mask sky and screen interiors. | Authors can follow painted contrast or reflected light. Specular glints can generate contours, so enable this channel only when requested. |
| Light terminator or band | For a chosen light, bracket zero in signed normal–light cosine, or bracket an authored threshold in that light's continuous response. Interpolate the crossing. Keep a change of shadow visibility as a separate input. | A dark paint stripe, a cast shadow and the end of direct illumination can receive three different treatments. |

**Identity needs work before object outlines will be reliable.** A mesh hit's visibility source is a staged draw index. The CPU draw has a picking identity and optional stable motion identity. A bake group can combine several instances, and instances can bake separately in the same frame. A transform slot, triangle number or change of draw cannot define the author's object. Map existing placement identities to view-local integer keys. For a merged mesh, carry the contributing logical part through the bake where the author distinguishes it, with explicit ownership on a blended field seam. Compare triangle-material lookup results independently. Exercise both a single object split into draws and two placements sharing one mesh. [sdfVisibilitySource](../../../src/Puck.SdfVm/Assets/Shaders/Sdf/frame/sdf-visibility.hlsli) · [SdfMeshDraw.Identity and MotionIdentity](../../../src/Puck.SdfVm/SdfMesh.cs) · [SdfMeshBakeGroup.InstanceIndices](../../../src/Puck.SdfVm/SdfMeshBakeGroup.cs) · [sdfMeshMaterial and sdfMeshResolveHit](../../../src/Puck.SdfVm/Assets/Shaders/Sdf/frame/sdf-mesh.hlsli)

**Lighting sets the earliest time to detect all the requested edges.** Visibility alone cannot tell which contour a shade program draws. The standard program computes its band from light cosine, wrap, visibility, threshold and softness. Let surface/light/compose publish a requested continuous scalar, with a specified light or authored reduction across lights. Keep uninked luminance available beside it. This avoids relighting neighbours, guessing a key light from final colour or detecting a line the ink drew itself. Extend the existing phase input and output tables and compiler admission with an ink phase that reads bounded edge data. Put weights, thresholds, width, colour and breakup in the standard package. ## Distance, cost and where the approximations show

A seed marks where a boundary crosses the image. Compare neighbouring samples once per horizontal or vertical pair, including tile seams. For discrete IDs, the midpoint is a first estimate within the bracket. For a continuous threshold use the interpolated crossing. Join compatible crossings in the cell into short segments, keeping both arms at corners and distinct segments at junctions. Distance to a segment avoids a row of round dots along a diagonal. Where the baked silhouette is under-sampled, a candidate can use the visible triangle's projected boundary to refine its crossing. Restrict refinement to confirmed contours to avoid exposing triangulation. Limn uses a subpixel seed and a short segment for a similar reason. A feature that misses every visibility sample needs finer visibility sampling or a geometry-generated contour. [Limn, The distance field](https://seoulmediastudio.com/docs/limn) · [Discontinuity Edge Overdraw](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/discontinuity-edge-overdraw/) · [sdfMeshResolveHit and sdfMeshBarycentric](../../../src/Puck.SdfVm/Assets/Shaders/Sdf/frame/sdf-mesh.hlsli)

Here is the arithmetic for **3,686,400 pixels at 2560 × 1440**. A completed nearest-seed field takes one lookup per query. Building it has a separate cost. The flood counts below assume a four-byte seed reference, two alternating buffers (28.125 MiB together), nine in-bounds candidate loads per pixel including the centre, and one store. They count logical accesses before cache reuse and omit seed generation, material data and composition. Boundaries reduce actual loads.

| Method | Derived work at this extent | Error and use |
|---|---|---|
| Full-view jump flooding (JFA) | 12 dispatches with strides 2048 down to 1. Up to 398.13 million candidate loads and 1.77 GB of logical reads and writes. Even one read and one write per pixel per pass represents 354 MB of buffer visits. | A winning seed can block the path of a seed needed farther away. One extra stride-1 pass often repairs errors and adds up to 147.46 MB. Neither variant guarantees exact distance or the correct material owner. [Rong and Tan](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~tants/jfa/i3d06.pdf) |
| Radius-limited flood | For an eight-pixel reach, strides 8, 4, 2, 1 cost up to 589.82 MB at full occupancy, before optional refinement. Restricting dispatch to a quarter of the tiles quarters these issued visits if all four passes use that list. | Pass count grows logarithmically with reach. Preserve intermediate tiles and clear or mask invalid seeds between dispatches. A half-resolution field saves work on broad strokes but can discard two nearby lines or move their owner. Refine from full-resolution seeds at the visible boundary. [Rong and Tan](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~tants/jfa/i3d06.pdf) · [Limn](https://seoulmediastudio.com/docs/limn) |
| Separable box dilation | For radius four, horizontal then vertical max over nine taps each costs 66.36 million mask reads and 7.37 million writes. At radius 16, 66 taps total cost 243.30 million reads. Counts assume a direct kernel. | A square mask gives Chebyshev distance as its reach. Its corner extends to √2 times the requested circular radius, 1.66 pixels too far at radius four. One dilation returns membership, without distance or winning owner. Use it for conservative tile reach. [OpenCV, Dilation](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/db/df6/tutorial_erosion_dilatation.html) |
| Exact Euclidean transform of grid seeds | Minimise squared distance along rows and then columns, carrying the winning coordinate. The separable lower-envelope algorithm takes work linear in pixel count. GPU parallel banding is an alternative for a full field. | Exact for the supplied point sites. A subpixel segment, per-owner exclusion or variable-width stroke changes the question. Use this as a reference for point seeds or when a full exact field pays. [Felzenszwalb and Huttenlocher, §2](https://cs.brown.edu/people/pfelzens/papers/dt-final.pdf) · [Parallel Banding Algorithm](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~tants/pba.html) |

The original flood paper's constant time is with respect to seed count on a fixed grid. It also reports small visible errors. Picking the wrong seed matters more when two seeds have different ink colours. A small CPU calculation illustrates the distinction. On an 8 × 8 grid, place seeds at (0,0), (1,0) and (4,4). At each stride, choose the closest of the nine previous-buffer candidates, breaking equal distances by lowest row-major seed index. At (6,0), strides 4, 2, 1 return squared distance 25 to (1,0). Exhaustive search gives 20 to (4,4). An extra unit pass repairs this case. This example is reproducible with three seeds and makes no claim about the frequency of errors in a scene. [Rong and Tan, JFA variants](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~tants/jfa/i3d06.pdf)

**First implementation.** Keep a short list or bitmask of occupied seed cells per tile. For a narrow stroke, load the tile and its required halo into shared memory and find the nearest eligible segment in the bounded neighbourhood. A fixed four-pixel radius gives a fixed upper bound independent of scene object count. Include the half-pixel coverage fringe and segment extent when choosing the halo. Test the simple implementation against exhaustive point-to-segment distances on diagonals, junctions and tile corners. Report distance in output pixels, the winning seed and edge class. Outside the authored reach, return an explicit out-of-range value. A material needing a larger radius expands the search or selects the wide path. Do not silently clip it.

**Classify destination tiles.** An 8 × 8 grid at native 1440p has 57,600 tiles. Reduce seed classes and conservative maximum reach per source tile. Mark every destination tile its expanded bounds touch, then compact the marked tiles into indirect dispatch arguments. A one-tile halo suffices for an eight-pixel reach only if the filter fringe and seed extent already fit within that reach. Within a marked tile reject uncovered pixels before invoking the author program. Supply the maximum reach from the program's admitted bound or an authored envelope that also bounds its evaluated width. With no finite proof, consider the whole view reachable. An empty view needs no ink resources, while a highly detailed view can use a direct dispatch when compaction costs more than it saves. The existing shade bins already count tiles, scatter tile IDs and generate indirect arguments. Share that machinery, keeping edge reach independent of the surface-shading bin. AMD uses dilated tile radii for depth of field, and Limn measures the same scheduling principle on outlines. ## Width, ownership and motion

Give the author two units. A screen-space radius in **output pixels** keeps a face readable at a distance. A radius in **metres** projects with the camera. For a perspective camera a camera-facing metre spans approximately `outputHeight / (2 × forwardDepth × tan(verticalFov/2))` pixels. Project a surface-tangent offset for a directionally foreshortened mark. Use the actual projection for off-axis views. Evaluate distance in the output metric by scaling each axis by output extent divided by render extent. Thus a four-output-pixel radius spans two render pixels at half linear resolution. Filter coverage across the projected footprint, including fractional widths. For very thin strokes integrate the overlap of both sides of the band so coverage tends to zero with width.

Width can taper with curvature, light or a mask evaluated at the seed in the object's rest frame. This lets a moving shield carry the same broken mark through the frame. World coordinates suit fixed terrain. A genuine end-to-end taper or a dash that follows a long contour needs an endpoint and distance *along* a connected stroke. Distance across it supplies neither. Where that matters, carry a curve parameter from the authored field or mesh, or chain visible segments and track them across frames. Start with authored coordinates before paying for chaining. Cole and Finkelstein's explicit stroke paths are a useful comparison for parameterised marks. [Fast High-Quality Line Visibility](https://gfx.cs.princeton.edu/pubs/Cole_2009_FHL/cole_2009_fhl.pdf) · [sdfMeshObjectPosition and sdfMeshBarycentric](../../../src/Puck.SdfVm/Assets/Shaders/Sdf/frame/sdf-mesh.hlsli)

At a depth break let the foreground surface speak, and clip any expansion behind nearer geometry. At a coplanar material boundary keep both material keys in one seed. Let each side opt in, declare a priority and choose inside, outside or centred placement. Resolve equal priorities with a stable semantic key, independent of draw order and thread order. Evaluate a centred stroke once. When several strokes reach a pixel, first test eligibility and actual radius for *each* candidate, then choose by authored priority, distance and stable key. A nearby disabled crease or a one-pixel seam must not hide a farther eight-pixel silhouette. A single nearest-any-edge texture loses this information. For wide floods carry a candidate per compatible class/owner where proven sufficient, and revisit intersecting tile lists wherever eligibility, priority or variable reach makes that shortcut ambiguous.

**Apply surface ink before temporal resolve.** Compute the seed in the current jittered sample frame and use the same sample frame for coverage. Leave ordinary inside strokes attached to the visible receiver. For a stroke extending outside it, supply the foreground seed's depth, motion and coverage separately from the background receiver, or restrict that stroke to the inside until resolve understands the extra layer. Reproject with the owning surface and validate identity, tangent-plane depth and current coverage. Reject stale samples on disocclusion, camera cuts or unrelated ownership. When resolution changes, transform both old and new coordinates through their own extents and jitters, or reset incompatible history. Limit history for a changing light band or refracted pattern. Marking every steady line reactive would throw away the subpixel samples that make a thin line stable. The current resolve already validates receiver identity and depth and reconstructs mesh motion from the previous pose. [sdfReprojection and sdfHistoryReceiverMatches](../../../src/Puck.SdfVm/Assets/Shaders/Sdf/frame/sdf-reprojection.hlsli) · [sdfResolveTemporal](../../../src/Puck.SdfVm/Assets/Shaders/Sdf/passes/sdf-resolve.comp.hlsl) · [SdfWorldPackage.HistorySurface and Reactivity](../../../src/Puck.Shaders.Model/Graph/SdfWorldPackage.Resolve.cs) · [AMD reactive-mask guidance](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/main/Kits/FidelityFX/docs/techniques/super-resolution-temporal.md)

The published placement advice differs for a reason. Epic shows roughly a pixel of jitter when a late silhouette is built from jittered geometry and recommends depth effects before temporal reconstruction. Limn defaults to a post-tonemap selection outline for exact display colour and uses unjittered custom depth or compensates and filters seed position. That choice suits a selection overlay. A late surface-ink path would need resolved edge position, owner, coverage and its own occlusion/temporal handling at output resolution. For a material in the world, preserve its relation to air, glass, exposure and bloom by shading it with the scene. ## Glass, water and views inside views

There are two different lines at a pond. A submerged stone owns its contour. Water bends, attenuates and can blur that already-inked light. The water surface can also own a line at its silhouette or an authored contact band. That line belongs at the interface and should follow its motion. A post-process over the final image cannot recover both owners. Today glass rasterisation writes the front hit and accumulates crossings up to the opaque hit. Glass shading can resolve a second front interface, samples the opaque lit image at the refracted position and composes far then near. Deeper layers contribute absorption within the existing representation. [Glass raster PSMain](../../../src/Puck.SdfVm/Assets/Shaders/Sdf/passes/sdf-mesh-glass.frag.hlsl) · [sdfGlassSecondInterface, sdfGlassLitAt and CSMain](../../../src/Puck.SdfVm/Assets/Shaders/Sdf/passes/sdf-glass-stage.hlsli) · [SdfWorldPackage.WithGlass](../../../src/Puck.Shaders.Model/Graph/SdfWorldPackage.Glass.cs)

For one detector per view, schedule glass visibility before detection. It needs opaque visibility for clipping. Where a glass program requests it, prepare its per-interface normal, material and authored scalar before the neighbour comparison. Give the detector separate opaque and interface inputs, including the second layer when represented. Match neighbouring interfaces by owner and depth order so a rear pane appearing beside a front pane stays distinct. Apply opaque ink before the refracted read and interface ink inside the existing far-to-near composition. Keep geometric coverage separate from ink opacity. If motion follows the opaque hit while a glass line moves independently, invalidate that contribution or give it interface motion. Individually inking three stacked interfaces requires more layer records. Optical-depth sums alone cannot locate their separate contours. [Glass graph construction](../../../src/Puck.Shaders.Model/Graph/SdfWorldPackage.Glass.cs) · [Glass shading CSMain](../../../src/Puck.SdfVm/Assets/Shaders/Sdf/passes/sdf-glass-stage.hlsli)

A portal's child view should detect at its own render extent and width scale. The parent may ink the frame geometry. A screen or opening's picture must be excluded as a source of parent luminance edges and as a destination for foreign strokes, except an explicitly owned frame stroke. Clamp neighbourhoods, tile lists and history to the view. Share no seed identity across the opening. Only scheduled views with ink demand pay, including when many places are visible inside one another. Puck already has projected screen coverage for sizing views and a separate shading path for a screen's picture. ## Recommended pipeline and 4070 budget

| Order | Implementation and check |
|---|---|
| 1. Prepare the view | Shade visible surfaces once. Leave scene colour before ink, grid and air available until ink is applied. Request only the light/contrast or glass signals used by admitted programs. Carry a semantic owner through grouped draws. Inspect a sloping floor, two copies of one creation, and two differently painted patches on one object in the debug world. Only the requested signals should light up. |
| 2. Detect once, then bound the search | One neighbourhood pass per enabled view emits subpixel boundary records for the requested layers and tile occupancy. Separate dispatches can propagate distance or build work lists. Compare the local narrow-band answer to exhaustive eligible segments. Start wider strokes with radius-limited JFA plus full-resolution refinement. Inspect owner-colour differences as well as distance error. Count tiles before and after dilation, candidate comparisons and actual ink invocations. Empty tiles should execute zero author programs. |
| 3. Apply, transport and resolve | Run the admitted ink phase only inside eligible bands. Produce scene-linear colour and line coverage, then finish opaque grid/air, refract it, composite interface ink, and resolve the view. Use distinct graph resource versions or a sparse overlay consumed by the next existing pass so a dispatch never samples a neighbour it might overwrite. A graph with no ink demand keeps the existing path. Compare a slow pan, moving actor, pane over water, changing render scale and nested portal. Look for a second frame line, swimming texture, width jumps and a trailing outline on the revealed background. |

The proposed invariant is that an authored stroke can read edges only from the current view and layer, before that stroke changed the colour. The view graph would enforce the ordering through immutable inputs. The admitted ink phase would expose those records through typed inputs and bound its reach for scheduling. Both pieces still need implementation. Sampled visibility sets the limit on discoverable detail. A stable owner and a valid silhouette sample are prerequisites for a stable distance, however accurate the propagation.

**Measured comparisons.** Seoul Media Studio reports **0.065 ms** for one two-pixel outline at 1920 × 1080 on an RTX 5070, down from **0.239 ms** before tiling. The reported stat is a median of 300 frames after 120 warm-up frames. Their three-object showcase took 0.068 ms after tonemapping and 0.091 ms before upscaling. This is a stencil-selected workload. Separately, the PBA authors report **0.88 ms** for an exact 2048² transform at 0.01% random seed density on a GTX 1080 Ti, rising to **1.26 ms** at 1%. These measure different passes and devices. Neither is a timing of Puck. [Limn performance](https://seoulmediastudio.com/docs/limn) · [PBA+, table 3](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~tants/pba.html)

**Expected incremental GPU cost per native 2560 × 1440 view on an RTX 4070: about 0.6–1.3 ms** for opaque depth, crease and ID edges, a short ink program, up to four pixels of reach and roughly 25% active tiles. This is a planning estimate for the new passes, before credit for retiring the current outline. The model charges roughly 118 MB for one effective sweep of the 32-byte visibility records and 15 MB for a four-byte seed-cell index per pixel. Budget another 20–50 MB for sparse segments, tile halos and colour access, assuming the finishing pass can consume the sparse result without a full-frame copy. At an **assumed** effective 200–320 GB/s, the resulting 153–183 MB takes 0.48–0.92 ms. Allow roughly 0.1–0.4 ms for classification, segment tests, the program and dispatch overhead. For scale, a GDDR6X 4070 with a 192-bit bus at 21 Gb/s per pin has a theoretical 504 GB/s. Effective bandwidth, tile occupancy, seed density, cache traffic and shader cost here are unmeasured. A separate RGBA16F read/write copy would add about 59 MB. Glass layers, dense foliage, broad competing strokes and multiple rendered views must be costed separately. [MSI RTX 4070 specification](https://www.msi.com/Graphics-Card/GeForce-RTX-4070-VENTUS-2X-12G-OC/Specification) · [Visibility layout, sdfVisibilityRecord](../../../src/Puck.SdfVm/Assets/Shaders/Sdf/frame/sdf-visibility.hlsli)

Have the implementation lane timestamp detection, tile setup, distance and authored ink separately on the debug-world route, with the 4070 at 2560 × 1440. Compare the current outline, the new graph with ink disabled, and the same graph with it enabled. Record the actual render extent, backend, topology, per-view active tiles, seed counts, maximum radius, glass layers and measured memory traffic beside median and tail GPU time. Repeat the moving view at reduced render scale and with the portal visible. Use those readings to decide the narrow/wide crossover and whether dense tiles should bypass compaction. The 4070 estimate remains unconfirmed until those measurements exist.
